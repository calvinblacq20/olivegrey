01 — The day

A day that fits a working family

Doors open at six in the morning and stay open until six in the evening. Children arriving early are fed, settled and looked after long before the first lesson, and nobody is hurried out at the end of it.

  • Early drop-off from 6:00, collection from 15:30
  • Late collection available until 18:00
  • Breakfast through lunch provided every day
Morning session

02 — Curriculum

Two curricula, one classroom

We teach the Ghana Education Service standards and Pearson Edexcel side by side rather than choosing between them. Lessons are activity based, so a child works something out before being told the answer.

  • Pearson Edexcel alongside the GES curriculum
  • Asante Twi and French from the early years
  • Computing, Abacus and Global Citizenship as subjects

OUR CURRICULUM

Activity based, from the first year to the last.

Two curricula taught side by side, three terms a year, from September through to August.

Numeracy

Numbers, counting and pattern handled as play before they are handled on paper, so the idea arrives before the notation does.

Literacy

Reading, writing, speaking and listening. A great deal of being read to, and a great deal of being listened to in return.

Sensorial

Shape, colour, texture, sound and movement — the groundwork that makes later abstraction possible.

Creativity

Drawing, painting, music and play, timetabled as work rather than offered as a reward for finishing something else.

Our World, Our People

Family, community and culture — where a child fits, and who else is in the picture with them.

English Language

Comprehension, composition and grammar across both the GES and Edexcel schemes, with every child writing something long each term.

Mathematics

Number, geometry and early algebra taught concrete first, alongside Abacus as a separate discipline in mental calculation.

Science

Practical work rather than demonstration — pupils set up the experiment, record what happened, and argue about why.

Computing

Keyboard fluency through to structured programming, feeding directly into the robotics club for those who want to go further.

Asante Twi & French

Two languages beside English from the early years. Twi is taught with the same seriousness as anything else on the timetable.

History & Global Citizenship

Ghana's story first, then the child's place in a wider world — what citizenship asks of them here and elsewhere.

Religious & Moral Education

The four values the school asks of every child — discipline, respect, excellence and responsibility — given somewhere to be discussed.

Crochet & Craft

Yarn, hooks and patience. Children work in small groups with a teacher alongside them, finishing pieces they take home.

Robotics

Building and programming machines that do something. The natural continuation of what starts in the computing room.

Ballet

Posture, discipline and performance, taught properly and shown to parents at the end of term.

Abacus

Mental arithmetic at speed. It runs as a club as well as a subject, for children who take to it.

Taekwondo

Control before contact. Belts are earned here, and children learn what it takes to be graded.

Cadet

Drill, teamwork and responsibility — the clearest place in the week where bearing and self-discipline are practised.

What we hold to

Four values we ask of every child

One

Discipline

Not silence in a corridor. The habit of finishing what you started, practised where it is hardest — in a taekwondo grading or a cadet drill.

Two

Respect

For the adults here, for each other, and for the two languages besides English that are spoken and taught in these rooms.

Three

Excellence

Two curricula held to at once, because meeting one standard well is easier than meeting both — and both is what we chose.

Four

Responsibility

A child who can be trusted with a task, a younger pupil, or a piece of equipment. It is taught by being given, not by being described.
